Your hands already know the way.

Cutefish is a Linux distribution designed with Mac users in mind. Its desktop is written from scratch in C++ and Skia, from the compositor to the built-in apps.

Light on memory, quick to open

Because the desktop, Dock and built-in apps all share one native toolkit, there is little else to load. Right after you log in, the whole system uses under 1 GB of memory, and the apps that come with Cutefish show their window in about a tenth of a second.

Built in the open

The Cutefish desktop and its ISO are built from public source by a public workflow, on a fresh machine that is thrown away afterwards. The package repository is signed by that workflow, and the key never sits on anyone's computer. You can check what you install instead of taking our word for it.

How to check
  • Built by a public GitHub Actions workflow, never on a developer's computer
  • Packages signed by that workflow, with a key kept off our computers
  • Every file traces back to the commit and the build that made it

Your Mac apps come along

Reef runs apps made for the Mac unchanged, with their menus in the menu bar and their icons in the Dock. Open one in Files and Reef installs with one click. CotEditor, NetNewsWire and Ghostty already work for everyday use.

About Reef

Agents can use it when you allow them

Claude Code, Codex and other agents that speak MCP can see the screen, click and type, through the compositor itself. Cutefish asks you first, a Stop button stays in the status bar while they work, and the moment you touch the pointer or keyboard they pause.
