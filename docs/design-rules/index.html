Design Rules

Layout rules for every Cutefish surface: shell, Settings, applications and toolkit controls. Sizes are in logical pixels unless they say device pixels.

Restraint

Each page is one clear thing with one hierarchy. Use one accent and add no decoration of your own: no glows, badges, illustrations or effects that the toolkit’s controls do not already draw.

Pixel grid

At fractional scales an odd logical coordinate falls between device pixels. Every edge lands on a whole device pixel:

  • Draw through Painter, which rounds each edge to the device grid at its absolute position, so boxes that share a logical edge share a device edge. Rounded shapes and icons snap their first edge and keep their size.
  • Borders, separators, insets and hairlines are whole device pixels, at least one. Use drawRoundedRect, strokeRoundedRect, drawHorizontalLine and drawVerticalLine, and ui::Icon for icons, never an antialiased rect at a fractional position.
  • Text sits on whole device pixels vertically.
  • Center content in its container by rounding each half on its own, so the two sides differ by at most one device pixel.

Layout tests check these at scales 1, 1.75 and 2.

Color

Colors come from the theme’s ColorId roles, which define the light and dark value of each role once; do not pick RGB values in a view. The accent is Highlight, the color the user chose in Settings. Destructive actions use the theme’s red.

Hairlines:

  • A separator between rows is one device pixel of ColorId::Separator, inset at both ends by the card’s horizontal padding.
  • The edge under a header and beside a sidebar is ColorId::HeaderSeparator, half a logical pixel thick: one device pixel with its alpha scaled down below 2x.

Corners

Use the theme’s radii: smallRadius 8 for fields and combo boxes, mediumRadius 10 for buttons and sidebar selections, bigRadius 12 for cards, windowRadius 14 for windows and menus.

Continuous corners suit large cards and panels, whose radius is small next to their size. A pill or a control whose radius is close to half its height keeps a circular curve; a continuous one flattens it into a lumpy square.

Insets in a rounded container

An element inset into a rounded window, panel or card, such as a hover or selection background, a tile or a footer row:

  1. Keeps the same gap to every container edge it touches.
  2. Takes the radius container radius - gap, so its corners stay concentric with the container’s. Continuous corners are subtracted the same way; a RoundedShape with an inset draws the parallel curve. When the result is zero or less, use the control’s own radius.

The background grows to meet the gap; text and icons stay at the container’s content padding.

For example, the Wi-Fi, Sound and Battery panels have a radius of 16. Their row backgrounds sit 6 from the sides, so their radius is 10, while row text stays 16 from the sides. A menu has a radius of 14 and a margin of 5, so its highlight has a radius of 9.

Cards and rows

  • A card has a radius of 12, a horizontal padding of 16 and a vertical padding of 12. Cards stand 12 apart.
  • A section label or footnote sits 8 from its card and shares the card’s horizontal padding, so every text edge on a page lines up.
  • A row is 44 high, 52 when it holds a slider or another control taller than a switch, and 56 with a second line of text.

Dialogs

  • A titled dialog is a ui::DialogWindow. Its title is 12 px, centered on the window and on the window controls’ line. Do not build a header by hand.
  • A sub-page, such as the IP settings of a network, replaces the content in the same window and shows the back button. It never opens a second window.
  • The content keeps 16 from the dialog’s edges, and blocks stand 16 apart.
  • Footer buttons stand 8 apart. The primary button is at the end; a destructive or extra button is at the start.
  • An alert without a title bar is a ui::ConfirmDialog: Cancel at the start, the confirming button at the end, the confirming button on top when they stack.

Menus and popups

  • Menus share one set of metrics: rows 26, separators 12, a margin of 5 on every side, text starting at 17 and a minimum width of 168. Do not change them per menu.
  • A menu button on the right of a title bar opens its menu directly under the button, its leading edge on the button’s plate (Menu::popupFor). Other menus open at the pointer.
  • Menus, panels and tooltips keep 6 from the bar they open from and from the screen edge.

Scroll bars

A scroll bar never runs into a rounded corner. Its ends stop ScrollBar::cornerPadding(radius) in, which keeps the handle’s end concentric with the corner and one inset clear of it. Never pick the padding by hand.

  • A scroll view that fills a rounded card or plate calls ScrollView::setCornerRadius(radius), which also clips the content.
  • A scroll view whose bottom edge is the window’s or panel’s calls ScrollView::setBottomCornerRadius(radius), which pads the bottom ends only.
  • A bar placed by hand uses ScrollBar::setPadding with cornerPadding, less any margin it already keeps above the edge.

A page’s scroll bar sits against the window’s right edge, and a dialog’s against the dialog’s: the scroll view spans the full width and the page margins become insets of its content.

A list in a side column keeps its bar at the column’s trailing edge. That edge meets the next column, not a window corner, so the bar keeps the default ScrollBar::inset at its ends and does not call setBottomCornerRadius.

Under a header, tab bar or other strip that content scrolls beneath, the track starts one inset below it: setVerticalBarPadding(header + ScrollBar::inset, ...). A bottom strip works the same way.

Tab bars

When tabs overflow, both ends of the bar stay round pills and tabs scrolling past are clipped by the pill’s curve, never cut flat. There is no edge fade, and wheel scrolling moves freely without snapping to tab edges. A short title keeps 12 from the tab’s sides, a long one 28, and hovering never changes how a title is elided.
